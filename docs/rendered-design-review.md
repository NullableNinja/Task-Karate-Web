# Rendered design review

This review is based on the rendered site in the browser, not only on source code. The Home, Programs, Schedule, About, Students, News, and Contact routes were inspected at desktop widths, including the visible schedule controls, student-resource interface, navigation, dialogs, scroll indicator, and the new location module.

## What is working

- The dark geometric background and Task-blue palette make the site recognizable as Task Karate and carry forward the strongest v3 visual memory.
- Real Task Karate photography gives the site credibility that generic martial-arts stock imagery would not.
- The program structure is understandable for kids, teens/adults, Eskrima, and sparring.
- The schedule is the strongest utility surface: program tracks, rank-first rows, day columns, compact pills, filters, and detail dialogs answer the practical question “when can I train?”
- The assistant-instructor list makes the family nature of the school visible instead of presenting Task as only one instructor.
- The student page now has a clear ownership boundary for rules, handbook guidance, testing downloads, and portal access.
- The v3-inspired eyebrows, geometric layering, belt-progress strip, and restrained gold accents give the site personality without introducing a separate visual gimmick.

## Weaknesses found and addressed

- Several pages used the same framed-panel rhythm with similar copy. A full-width location band and a map preview now create a different, more useful visual beat.
- The location was previously just an address or outbound directions link. Home and Contact now show a visible OpenStreetMap preview, a gold location marker, directions, and an interactive-map link.
- Contact copy leaned too heavily toward a parent calling about a child. It now explicitly includes kids, teens, adults, and Eskrima without assuming who is starting.
- The home page repeated the About headline and instructor biography too closely. The home page now sells the small-school difference and links to About for the deeper story.
- Programs copy previously sounded like a second set of rules. It now tells visitors how to choose a program and what they will find on each detail page.
- The schedule displayed historical 2025 effective dates without a current replacement. The note now clearly identifies the schedule as informational and tells visitors to confirm current details.
- Local-business structured data now includes map coordinates and a map URL, supporting local discovery without inventing opening hours.
- The first location embed rendered blank in the local browser, so it was replaced with a visible, lazy-loaded, attributed tile preview that still links to a full interactive map.

## Research translated into Task-specific decisions

Current martial-arts website reviews consistently reward real student photography, clear age/program identification, a schedule that is one click away, instructor credibility, and a low-friction first-class path. Task now uses those patterns while retaining its own Task-blue/v3 identity rather than copying a franchise or template.
